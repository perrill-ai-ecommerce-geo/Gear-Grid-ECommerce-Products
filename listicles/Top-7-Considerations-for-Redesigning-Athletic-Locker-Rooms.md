# Top 7 Considerations for Redesigning Athletic Locker Rooms

Redesigning a locker room is not just about replacing old lockers. It means improving storage, traffic, shower access, benches, airflow, security, branding, and the daily routine of every person who uses the space. Poor design creates clutter, odors, slow gear drying, safety issues, and frustrated staff.

Successful athletic facilities balance functionality, durability, and team culture. The best project decisions are based on how athletes arrive, change, drop gear, store items, recover, and leave ready for work or competition.

## Top 7 Considerations for Athletic Locker Room Redesign

### 1. Space Planning and Layout Optimization

Space planning is the foundation. Locker room layouts must ensure proper traffic flow, enough walking room, and accessible routes. ADA guidelines require 48 inches of space around accessible lockers, and 5% of lockers must be ADA compliant in facilities; 5% of lockers must be ADA compliant in design.

Why it stands out: good layout maximizes limited floor area and keeps athletes from crowding one station.

Best for: multi-sport teams, high-traffic rooms, or buildings where nothing can be left on the ground.

Key strengths: better flow, more storage, safer access, and flexible configurations, especially when you incorporate [athletic and education storage solutions](https://www.geargrid.com/sports-athletic-storage-solutions/) that are designed for busy sports facilities.

Possible limitations: expert measurement and detailed planning are needed, and many design teams rely on [architect Revit files for GearGrid lockers](https://www.geargrid.com/resources/architect-drawing-blocks/) to coordinate clearances and utilities accurately, especially when integrating [tactical standard wall mount lockers](https://www.geargrid.com/product/standard-tactical-wall-mount-lockers/) or other specialty systems into multi-use facilities.

### 2. Locker Material Selection and Durability

Material choice controls durability, cleaning time, and replacement cost. Metal lockers account for the majority of U.S. locker sales. Wood lockers are extremely durable and furniture quality. Wood veneer lockers are more economical than solid wood lockers. Plastic lockers are water resistant and do not rust. Plastic laminate lockers can be made from various materials.

The flame-spread rating is determined by ASTM Test Method E-84, and the flame spread rating determines locker material fire safety. GearGrid’s [athletic lockers manufacturing process](https://www.geargrid.com/how-its-made-athletic-lockers/) uses steel wire and tube, hand-applied powder coat, and curing at 350–400°F.

Best for: moisture-heavy locker rooms and facilities that need a durable product.

Possible limitations: premium materials cost more upfront, particularly when adding secure elements like [customizable fire locker accessories](https://www.geargrid.com/blog/customize-geargrids-fire-lockers-innovative-accessories/) or [dedicated SecureBox compartments](https://www.geargrid.com/product/securebox-fire-ems/) for valuables and sensitive gear.

### 3. Team Workflow and Accessibility

A room should match how people move. Place shelves, drawers, benches, and shower routes where they reduce delays. Full length lockers are typically 42 inches tall, and lockers can include features like built-in benches and drawers.

Locker room seating must include at least one ADA compliant bench. ADA compliant locks must be operable with one hand.

Best for: programs serving different teams, ages, and accessibility needs.

Key strengths: smoother routines, inclusive use, and less worry for coaches managing equipment when you apply practical [athletic locker room organization tips](https://www.geargrid.com/athletic-locker-room-organization-tips-for-coaches-and-players/) and, where appropriate, specialized [storage accessories that enhance organization and safety](https://www.geargrid.com/product-category/fire-ems-products/fire-ems-storage-accessories/) that support daily habits.

Possible limitations: workflow planning may change the number, size, or position of lockers.

### 5. Ventilation and Moisture Management

Moisture destroys gear and creates odor. Open-grid designs help air move around helmets, pads, uniforms, and personal items, and dedicated [helmet and shoulder pad storage solutions](https://www.geargrid.com/post_storage/helmet-shoulder-pad-storage/) further improve drying and organization, especially when you add [GearGrid GearTopper accessories above lockers](https://www.geargrid.com/product/geartopper/) to keep helmets and pads elevated. Climate control is crucial for storing electronics and documents to prevent degradation.

Storage lockers are small enclosed spaces designed for storing compact items, but athletic lockers need more airflow because damp equipment is used every day. Storage lockers should not be used for storing perishables or hazardous materials.

Best for: hockey, football, aquatics, and high-humidity facilities.

Key strengths: improved health, faster drying, reduced odor, and easier cleaning, especially when paired with [specialized equipment storage systems for athletic gear](https://www.geargrid.com/product-category/athletic-products/athletic-equipment-storage/) that keep items organized and exposed to airflow.

## How to Prioritize Your Locker Room Redesign Factors

### Prioritize Based on Facility Usage Patterns

Start with a check of peak time, traffic, wet zones, gear volume, and who uses the room. Then consider [locker room organization strategies for coaches and players](https://www.geargrid.com/blog/athletic-locker-room-organization-tips-for-coaches-and-players/) that align with those patterns. Storage lockers are suitable for storing seasonal items, sports equipment, and personal items during moves. Businesses can use storage lockers for organized and secure storage of equipment and documents, while fire and EMS agencies may prefer a [Mini Mobile Storage System for Fire & EMS](https://www.geargrid.com/product/mini-mobile-storage-system-fire-ems/) that keeps mission-critical gear close at hand. Storage lockers help in reducing clutter by providing space for office supplies and personal items.

### Prioritize Based on Budget and Timeline

Storage lockers are ideal for budget-conscious users. Storage lockers are ideal for residents of small apartments requiring off-site storage. Lockers are cost-effective storage solutions compared to full-sized units. Storage lockers typically act as a lower-cost alternative to full-sized storage units, while purpose-built [standard wall mount lockers for Fire & EMS](https://www.geargrid.com/product/fire-ems-wall-mount-lockers/) or [tactical personnel mobile lockers](https://www.geargrid.com/product/personnel-tactical-mobile-lockers/) illustrate how specialty designs can justify higher upfront investment through durability and performance.

Before you order, remember: choosing the right storage locker requires balancing protection, accessibility, and cost.

### Prioritize Based on Program Goals

If culture matters, invest in finishes and branding. If safety matters, prioritize open sightlines and secure lock options. If maintenance matters, choose materials that clean quickly and resist moisture.

Facility location is important for convenience based on how often locker access is needed. Access hours and drive-up access significantly affect the usability of storage facilities, especially when a convenient location determines whether clients can find and retrieve items on schedule.

## Which Approach Is Best for Your Facility?

Choose space planning if the room feels crowded. Choose material selection if the main issue is repair, rust, or cleaning. Choose workflow optimization if multiple teams use the same locker room. Choose modular solutions if you need to accommodate change.
