# 5 Things to Consider When Choosing Mobile Lockers

## 1. Confirm that your storage needs to move.

Mobile lockers are most useful when a space has to change. Examples include multi-use rooms shared by several teams or crews, equipment areas that get rearranged by season, and facilities where floors need regular cleaning under and behind storage. If gear will stay in one place and wall space is available, a fixed wall-mounted or freestanding layout may be the simpler choice. Start by deciding how often the storage will move and why.

## 2. Plan for floor surfaces, travel paths, and staying in place.

Mobility depends on the space around the locker as well as the locker itself. Check the floor surface, slopes, thresholds, and any drains the lockers will roll over. Make sure aisles and doorways are wide enough for loaded units. Also plan how the lockers will stay put once they are in position, so they don't shift while people are pulling gear in and out during a busy shift change or practice.

## 3. Match the locker style to the gear's airflow and visibility needs.

Gear that comes back wet or sweaty, such as turnout gear, athletic pads and uniforms, PPE, and field clothing, is easier to manage in open or ventilated storage that lets air circulate around it. Open-grid designs also show at a glance what is stocked and what is missing. Door-style storage may be the better fit where items need to be closed off or kept out of view. Choose based on what is being stored, not on appearance alone.

## 4. Decide between personal and shared storage.

Some facilities assign one mobile locker per person. Others use mobile units as shared storage for a team, crew, or shift. Personal lockers make each person accountable for their own gear. Shared units work well for seasonal equipment, overflow gear, or items that move between rooms with a group. Knowing who will use each locker shapes how many you need, how they are labeled, and which accessories make sense.

## 5. Verify dimensions, accessories, and site conditions before ordering.

Before ordering, confirm the locker's exact size against the space where it will park and the routes it will travel. Check which accessories are needed, such as shelves, hooks, or nameplates, and whether the loaded weight will suit the floor and how often the locker will move. Confirming these details with the manufacturer early helps the storage fit the space as planned and avoids surprises at delivery.
