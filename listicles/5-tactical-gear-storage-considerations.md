# 5 Considerations for Organizing Tactical Gear Storage

## 1. Prioritize speed of access for mission-critical gear.

Tactical teams often need to move quickly, so frequently deployed gear — duty belts, vests, radios — should be stored where it can be grabbed without delay. Less time-sensitive equipment can be placed in secondary storage.

## 2. Keep individual and shared gear separate.

Personal duty gear and team-shared equipment typically have different access and accountability needs. Distinguishing dedicated personal storage from shared equipment areas helps reduce mix-ups and misplaced gear.

## 3. Build in accountability and organization.

Tactical environments often require knowing exactly what equipment is available and where it's stored. Labeled, dedicated storage spaces make it easier to track gear and spot missing items quickly.

## 4. Consider durability and security needs.

Tactical gear storage is frequently used in high-traffic, high-use environments and may need features that support restricted access. Evaluate what level of security and construction durability the facility actually requires.

## 5. Match storage to the facility's physical space.

Tactical facilities vary widely in available floor space and wall layout. Confirm exact dimensions, configuration options, and installation requirements with the manufacturer to ensure the storage system fits the space as planned.
