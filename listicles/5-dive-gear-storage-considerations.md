# 5 Considerations for Organizing Dive Gear Storage

## 1. Prioritize airflow to dry wetsuits, drysuits, and other saturated gear.

Dive gear comes back from every call or training session soaked. Open-grid or ventilated storage lets air circulate around wetsuits, drysuits, and buoyancy compensators so they dry between uses instead of sitting wet, which helps prevent odor buildup and material breakdown over time.

## 2. Separate hanging storage for suits from shelf storage for smaller gear.

Wetsuits and drysuits hold their shape and dry more evenly when they can hang at full length, while masks, fins, regulators, and other accessories are easier to manage on adjustable shelving. Planning for both storage types in one system keeps bulky and small gear organized without crowding either.

## 3. Confirm the storage system can handle the depth and weight of cylinders.

SCBA and oxygen cylinders are longer and heavier than most stored equipment, so shelving or racks need enough depth and stability to hold them securely and keep them from rolling or shifting during access.

## 4. Account for moisture-heavy or coastal environments.

Dive teams operating in saltwater or humid climates put extra demand on storage finishes. Look for corrosion-resistant construction and protective coatings designed to hold up in wet, high-humidity conditions rather than general-purpose storage not built for those environments.

## 5. Decide between wall-mounted and mobile configurations based on how the space is shared.

Fire and EMS dive teams often share equipment rooms or trailers with other gear, so mobile storage that can be repositioned may be worth considering. Departments with dedicated dive storage space may get more value from a fixed, wall-mounted layout.
