# 5 Public Works Gear Storage Planning Tips

## 1. Inventory PPE, uniforms, tools, and daily-use field equipment before selecting a storage system.

Starting with a complete inventory of what your crew needs to store — from high-visibility vests and hard hats to hand tools and seasonal equipment — helps match the storage system to actual daily operations instead of choosing based on appearance or available floor space alone.

## 2. Identify which gear is needed every shift and which equipment can be stored in secondary areas.

Not all equipment is reached at the same frequency. Separating high-use daily gear from seasonal or low-frequency items allows your primary storage area to stay organized and accessible during morning staging and shift transitions.

## 3. Consider mobile storage for departments with shared spaces or layouts that change over time.

Public works facilities often share space with multiple crews or reconfigure their work areas as staffing and operations evolve. Mobile lockers can be repositioned without reinstallation and can make routine cleaning and maintenance easier to manage.

## 4. Use airflow-friendly storage where field gear regularly comes back wet or dirty.

Crews returning from outdoor fieldwork often bring back damp PPE, muddy boots, and wet uniforms. Open or ventilated storage supports airflow around frequently wet gear and improves visibility into what is clean, dry, and ready for the next shift.

## 5. Verify dimensions, accessories, and site conditions before ordering.

Public works facilities vary widely in available wall space, ceiling height, floor layout, and infrastructure. Confirming exact dimensions, mounting requirements, and accessory needs before placing an order helps avoid installation delays and ensures the storage system fits the space as planned.
